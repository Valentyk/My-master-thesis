# My-master-thesis

In this repository, I share my master's thesis, supervised by Dražen Glavan, PhD, titled "Resonant particle production of an evolving oscillating scalar field", which I wrote for my master's studies of Theoretical physics at Charles University. Besides the thesis itself, you can find here the reports from my supervisor (in English) and reviewer (in Czech).

The thesis describes a simple model of particle production in an early Universe. This process (in a simplified case) can be described by the Mathieu equation 

$$\ddot{u}(t) + \left[\kappa^2 + \lambda \phi^2 \cos^2{(\omega t)}\right] u(t) = 0.$$

This is a differential equation with periodic coefficients. Essentially, the task of the thesis was to find the solutions $u(t)$ in a more general case where the amplitude $\phi(t)$ and frequency $\omega(t)$ are functions of time. In the thesis, I introduce an approximate method of multiple-scale analysis which I then use to derive approximate solutions to the "time-dependent" Mathieu equation

$$\ddot{u} + \left[\kappa^2 + \lambda \phi(t)^2 \cos^2{(\alpha(t))}\right] u = 0, \qquad \text{where} \quad \alpha(t) = \int \mathrm{d}t' \omega(t).$$

The work is rather technical, but I tried to explain everything in detail so the reader does not get lost. Feel free to have a look!
